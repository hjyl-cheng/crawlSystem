Recommended weights for determining the channel's topics:

If the channel does not have enough content from the most recent 180 days, analyze its 20 most recently published items without applying a publication-date limit.

Step 3: Analyze video text and visuals

Check as much of the following as possible:

Read at least 30-50 valid comments whenever possible, while including:

- Top comments
- Recent comments
- Pinned comments

- Slang
- Life stage
- Occupation
- Family status
- Product needs
- Regional context
- Purpose for using the content

Step 5: Establish internal evidence levels

For every field, distinguish internally between:

A. Explicit evidence:

Information directly and publicly stated by the channel or creator.

B. Strong evidence:

Consistent information repeatedly appearing across multiple videos, subtitles, visuals, or comments.

C. Medium evidence:

A combination of signals from language, accent, currency, location, brands, lifestyle, and content topics.

D. Weak evidence:

An inference from a single video, single comment, single thumbnail, or single name.

Priority of use:

Explicit evidence > multiple consistent strong signals > multiple medium signals > one weak signal

Do not allow one video, one comment, or one thumbnail to override the channel's long-term pattern.

If different evidence conflicts:

1. Prioritize newer evidence.
2. Prioritize evidence directly expressed by the creator.
3. Prioritize evidence repeated across multiple videos.
4. When comment samples conflict with video content, comprehensively account for commenter bias and do not mechanically adopt the comment proportions.
5. Ultimately, you must select one specific value that is most likely and must not output an explanation of the conflict.

13. The `active_subscriber_ratio` Field
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The field `active_subscriber_ratio` represents:

“The estimated percentage of total subscribers who, during the most recent 30 days, watched, liked, commented, shared, or otherwise interacted at least once.”

It must output one specific integer from 0 to 100.

Do not output a percent sign.

The following estimation must be performed internally. Do not output the calculation process in the final result.

Step 1: Determine the channel's subscriber-size tier

Prioritize reading the public `subscriberCount`.

If the subscriber count is hidden:

- Estimate from visible channel view counts
- Number of videos
- Number of comments
- Number of likes
- Historical content performance
- Upload frequency
- Public YouTube channel summaries

Estimate whether the channel belongs to:

- Fewer than 50,000 subscribers
- 50,000 to 500,000 subscribers
- More than 500,000 subscribers

Do not skip the calculation merely because the subscriber count is hidden.

Step 2: Calculate typical recent views

Prioritize the newest 10-12 videos published within the most recent 90 days.

If there are not enough videos, use all recent videos.

Do not directly use the single video with the highest view count.

Prioritize using:

- Median
- Trimmed mean after removing extreme values
- The stable level across multiple recent items

Clearly abnormal viral videos should be downweighted or excluded.

If a video's view count is more than 3 times the recent median, check whether it is:

- A viral hit
- Search traffic
- A news trend
- Shorts recommendations
- A sudden increase in an old video's views
- An outlier outside the channel's normal performance

Long-form videos and Shorts must be observed separately.

If the channel publishes both long-form videos and Shorts:

- Estimate typical views separately for each
- Apply combined weighting based on publication count and view contribution during the most recent 30 days
- Do not directly equate high Shorts views with subscriber activity

Step 3: Calculate the base ratio

Calculate internally:

Typical recent views per video ÷ total subscribers × 100

This base ratio is only a single-video views-to-subscribers ratio and cannot be used directly as `active_subscriber_ratio`.

It is strictly prohibited to place a single-video views-to-subscribers ratio such as 2%, 3%, or 5% directly into the final field.

Step 4: Apply 30-day time-window scaling

Because one subscriber may watch multiple videos within 30 days, deduplicated monthly active subscribers are usually higher than the views-to-subscribers ratio of one video.

Multiply the base ratio by 3 to 5 according to upload frequency and audience overlap:

- Fewer uploads and highly overlapping video audiences: approximately 3 times
- Stable weekly uploads: approximately 3.5 to 4 times
- High-frequency uploads where different content reaches different audiences: approximately 4 to 5 times
- High-frequency publication of many similar Shorts: do not use the highest multiplier merely because there are many videos
- The greater the content repetition and viewer overlap, the lower the multiplier should be

Step 5: Channel-size adjustment

- Fewer than 50,000 subscribers:
  The size adjustment may be close to 1.0; small and midsize channels usually have stronger subscriber loyalty.
- 50,000 to 500,000 subscribers:
  Multiply by approximately 0.8 to 0.9.
- More than 500,000 subscribers:
  Multiply by approximately 0.6 to 0.7; large channels usually have more historically accumulated subscribers.

Step 6: Traffic-quality adjustment

Adjust downward for:

- Shorts-driven viral traffic
- One video far outperforming all others
- A high share of search traffic
- A high share of recommendation traffic
- Large amounts of casual non-subscriber viewing
- Comedy clips
- Film or television clips
- Music compilations
- Children's songs or repeat-play content
- Temporary traffic caused by a news trend
- Large historical subscriber accumulation from old viral hits
- Recent views clearly below historical levels
- Very few comments
- Long-term inactivity
- Extremely irregular uploads

Adjust moderately upward for:

- Highly focused content
- Stable uploads
- Serialized content
- Many returning viewers in comments
- Stable views across multiple recent videos
- Frequent creator-audience interaction
- Tutorial channels
- Technology channels
- Software channels
- Career-skills channels
- Personal Vlogs
- High rewatch demand
- Active Community section
- Clear motivation to subscribe

Step 7: Anchor the final range

Based on the channel's overall performance, anchor the result within a reasonable range:

- Excellent, highly loyal channel: 45 to 75
- Normally healthy channel: 25 to 44
- Low-activity, old, or broad-entertainment channel: 8 to 24
- Long-term inactive, abnormally low-interaction, or clearly burdened by a large amount of accumulated inactive subscribers: 0 to 7

Evaluation criteria:

Excellent, highly loyal channel:

- Frequent uploads
- High content focus
- Stable views across multiple recent videos
- Many returning viewers in comments
- Strong motivation to subscribe

Normally healthy channel:

- Regular uploads
- Relatively stable views
- Ongoing comments and interaction
- No severe inactivity or dependence on viral hits

Low-activity or old channel:

- Slow uploads
- Recent views clearly below historical levels
- Dependence on old videos
- More casual non-subscriber traffic
- Viral Shorts but relatively weak subscriber loyalty

Anomalous or zombie-subscriber channel:

- Long-term inactivity
- Almost no recent views or comments
- A severe mismatch between subscriber count and recent performance

Additional rules:

- When no content was published during the most recent 30 days, do not give a high activity ratio solely from historical viral videos.
- When the channel has been inactive for several recent months, the result should be reduced substantially.
- Do not directly use like rate, comment rate, or interaction rate as `active_subscriber_ratio`.
- Do not output a value close to 100 merely because one video's views exceed the subscriber count.
- The final value must be one specific integer.

14. The `channel_tags` Field
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The field `channel_tags` must be an object and may contain only:

1. tags
2. top_5_distribution

Format:

{
  "tags": [...],
  "top_5_distribution": [...]
}

Rules for `tags`:

- Output exactly 10 English content tags.
- There must be exactly 10 tags.
- Tags must not be duplicated.
- Tags must describe the channel's:
  - Content domain
  - Subject matter
  - Vertical industry
  - Video type
  - Stable creation style
- Tags should be as specific as possible.
Examples of appropriate directions:

- "Python Tutorial"
- "Mobile Game Strategy"
- "Home Cooking"
- "Smartphone Review"
- "Hair Tutorial"
- "Travel Guide"
- "Car Repair"
- "AI Tools"
- "Skincare Routine"
- "Pet Care"

Do not use overly generic tags:

- "Content"
- "Videos"
- "YouTube"
- "Entertainment"
- "Creator"
- "Interesting"
- "Popular"
- "Social Media"

Tag ordering:

- Tags 1 through 5 must be the 5 most central tags.
- Tags 1 through 5 must be ordered from highest to lowest importance.
- Tags 6 through 10 are secondary topics that nevertheless appear consistently.
- A topic appearing only once or twice incidentally must not be included.
- Recent content takes priority over early historical content.

Rules for `top_5_distribution`:

- Output exactly 6 items.
- The `tag` values of the first 5 items must exactly match, character for character, the first 5 items in the `tags` array.
- Their order must match the first 5 items in `tags`.
- The sixth item must be fixed as:
  {"tag": "Other", "percentage": integer}
- The sum of the 6 `percentage` values must equal exactly 100.
- Every `percentage` must be an integer.
- The first 5 weights must be ordered from highest to lowest importance.
- The item whose `tag` value is `Other` represents the combined share of tags 6 through 10 and other secondary subjects.
- Do not apply the same percentage distribution to every channel.

Basis for determining weights:

- Number of videos for each topic during the most recent 90 days
- Upload frequency for each topic during the most recent 180 days
- Each topic's contribution to views
- Each topic's contribution to comment interaction
- Core positioning in the channel description
- Content series that continue to be updated

16. Method for Selecting `level_1`
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

When selecting `level_1`, determine:

“What do viewers primarily want to watch, learn, experience, or solve when they consume most of this channel's recent content?”

Priority order:

1. The topic with the highest share of video count during the most recent 90 days.
2. The topic contributing the most views during the most recent 90 days.
3. The topic updated most consistently during the most recent 180 days.
4. The clearest channel positioning in the channel description.
5. A core series that has continued over the long term.
6. The primary viewing need repeatedly expressed in the comment section.

If two `level_1` categories are close:

1. Select the category with the higher share during the most recent 90 days.
2. If still close, select the category contributing more views.
3. If still close, select the category updated more consistently.
4. If still close, select the category that covers more core videos.
5. Ultimately, output only one `level_1`; ties are not allowed.

17. Easily Confused Classification Boundaries
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. `Beauty Creators` vs. `Fashion`

Select `Beauty Creators` when:

- The core is the person's attractiveness, charm, appearance display, or visual appreciation.
- Viewers primarily focus on the person appearing on screen.
- Videos center on handsome men, beautiful women, personal image, portraits, or charm-focused edits.
- Makeup, skincare, outfits, or fashion-product knowledge is not the main subject.

Select `Fashion` when:

- The core is makeup, skincare, clothing, footwear, bags, accessories, hairstyles, wigs, or fashion information.
- Viewers primarily want to learn about, review, purchase, or understand fashion content.

Do not select `Beauty Creators` merely because the creator is attractive or handsome.

2. `Beauty Creators` vs. `Casual Vlogs`

- Pure display of a person's attractiveness or charm: `Beauty Creators`.
- Everyday clips, life records, or ordinary short clips of people: `Casual Vlogs`.
- Primarily portrait clips without emphasizing a handsome-men or beautiful-women theme: `Casual Vlogs` → `Portrait Clips`.

3. `Fashion` vs. `Health & Wellness`

- Makeup: `Fashion` → `Makeup`.
- Beauty-oriented skincare: `Fashion` → `Skincare`.
- Personal health and care that is not beauty-oriented: `Health & Wellness` → `Personal Health & Care`.
- Oral care: `Health & Wellness` → `Oral Care`.
- Nutritional supplements: `Health & Wellness` → `Supplements`.
- Sexual-health content: `Health & Wellness` → `Sexual Health & Wellness`.

4. Boundaries within `Fashion`

- Makeup tutorials and cosmetics products: `Makeup`.
- Skincare routines and skincare products: `Skincare`.
- Clothing and outfit styling: `Clothing`.
- Shoes: `Footwear`.
- Bags and luggage: `Bags & Luggage`.
- Jewelry, watches, belts, glasses, and similar items: `Accessories`.
- Underwear, sleepwear, and loungewear: `Underwear & Loungewear`.
- Tattoos: `Tattoo`.
- Hairstyles, hair extensions, and wigs: `Hair & Wigs`.
- Fashion news and industry developments: `Fashion News`.
- A mixture of multiple fashion and accessory topics that cannot be summarized by one product category: `Fashion & Accessories`.
- Content that genuinely belongs to fashion but cannot fit a more specific category: `Other Fashion`.

5. `Casual Vlogs` vs. `Travel`

- Everyday-life records where travel is only an incidental setting: `Casual Vlogs`.
- Destinations, routes, accommodation, attractions, or travel experiences as the core: `Travel`.
- Records of the travel process: `Travel Vlogs`.
- Routes, transportation, costs, visas, and travel strategies: `Travel Guides`.
- Explanations of local attractions or culture: `Tour Guide Content`.
- Hotels, homestays, and lodging experiences: `Hotels & Stays`.
- Travel photography works: `Travel Photography`.

6. `Photography` vs. `Photography Equipment`

- Photography works, photography techniques, and the shooting process: `Casual Vlogs` → `Photography`.
- Reviews of cameras, lenses, lighting, stabilizers, and similar equipment: `Tech` → `Photography Equipment`.

7. `Tech` vs. `Software & Internet`

Select `Tech` when:

- The core subjects are physical hardware, digital products, or electronic devices.
- This includes phones, computers, cameras, headphones, televisions, smart devices, and consumer electronics.
- The focus is unboxing, reviews, specifications, comparisons, or purchase advice.

Select `Software & Internet` when:

- The core subjects are software, Apps, AI tools, internet services, or Web3.
- The focus is software operation, AI workflows, app tutorials, or platform features.

Specific distinctions:

- Phone hardware: `Tech` → `Mobile Tech`.
- Mobile Apps: `Software & Internet` → `Mobile Apps`.
- Computers and PC hardware: `Tech` → `Computers & PCs`.
- Desktop software: `Software & Internet` → `Computer Software`.
- AI tools and models: `Software & Internet` → `Artificial Intelligence`.
- Cameras and lenses: `Tech` → `Photography Equipment`.
- Technology news: `Tech` → `Tech News`.

8. Boundaries within `Tech`

- Phones and tablets: `Mobile Tech`.
- Televisions, headphones, audio systems, and home electronics: `Consumer Electronics`.
- Desktop computers, laptops, and computer accessories: `Computers & PCs`.
- Novel small devices, portable devices, and compact hardware: `Gadgets & Devices`.
- Broad digital hardware that cannot fit a more specific category: `Digital Devices`.
- Technology-industry and product news: `Tech News`.
- Cameras, lenses, and photography hardware: `Photography Equipment`.

9. `Screen Recordings` vs. `Software & Internet`

- Pure screen recordings, chat logs, web browsing, or reaction content without clear software instruction: `Casual Vlogs` → `Screen Recordings`.
- App, AI, software, or platform tutorials: `Software & Internet`.
- Do not automatically select `Screen Recordings` merely because the visual content is a screen recording.

10. `Gaming` vs. `Tech`

- Gameplay, guides, mechanics, and stories: `Gaming`.
- Content primarily reviewing game consoles, controllers, keyboards, mice, and gaming devices: `Gaming` → `Gaming Hardware & Accessories`.
- A general computer-hardware channel that occasionally runs gaming tests: `Tech` → `Computers & PCs`.
- When the core is PC building, graphics cards, or hardware performance, do not select `Gaming` merely because games appear on screen.

11. Boundaries within `Gaming`

- Tactics, resource management, business simulation, and strategic planning: `Strategy Games`.
- Exploration, puzzles, and story-driven adventure: `Adventure Games`.
- Shooting, fighting, and fast-paced combat: `Action Games`.
- Character progression, equipment, classes, and role-playing: `Role-Playing Games`.
- Light, casual, puzzle, and party games: `Casual Games`.
- Content mainly centered on mobile and tablet games: `Mobile Games`.
- Gaming hardware, consoles, and accessories: `Gaming Hardware & Accessories`.

The same game may belong to multiple genres. Determine the category from the channel's actual content focus; do not mechanically copy game-store labels.

12. `Food` vs. `Home`

- Recipes and cooking steps: `Food` → `Cooking Tutorials`.
- Plating, visual preparation processes, and dish presentation: `Food` → `Food Presentation`.
- Tastings, restaurant reviews, or food evaluations: `Food` → `Food Reviews`.
- Mukbang: `Food` → `Mukbang`.
- Wilderness or outdoor cooking: `Food` → `Wilderness Cooking`.
- Ingredient knowledge, food culture, and food science: `Food` → `Food Knowledge`.
- Reviews of kitchen appliances, cookware, and tableware: `Home` → `Kitchen Appliances & Dining`.

13. `Home` vs. `Tech`

- Furniture, household appliances, interior arrangement, kitchen items, and hardware tools: `Home`.
- Phones, computers, digital devices, and technology products: `Tech`.
- Smart-home content focused on technology functions and hardware reviews: `Tech`.
- Smart-home content focused on household use, arrangement, and lifestyle improvement: `Home`.

14. `Home` vs. `Automotive`

- General household tools, power drills, saws, and hardware: `Home` → `Tools & Hardware`.
- Auto parts, repair tools, and in-car devices: `Automotive`.
- The auto-repair process: `Automotive` → `Auto Repair`.
- Car cleaning, detailing, and exterior modification: `Automotive` → `Car Care & Styling`.

15. `Education` vs. `Self Improvement`

- School courses, student life, campuses, and exams: `Education`.
- Job seeking, communication, and professional abilities: `Self Improvement` → `Career Skills`.
- Emotions, psychology, and interpersonal relationships: `Self Improvement` → `Emotions & Psychology`.
- Do not select `Education` for all videos merely because they use an instructional format.

16. Boundaries within `Education`

- School-subject courses and K-12 academic instruction: `K-12 Education`.
- Primary- and secondary-school stages, school systems, and content related to primary or secondary students: `Primary & Secondary School`.
- School news, notices, and events: `School News`.
- Other campus topics: `Other Campus Content`.
- Students' daily routines and campus life: `Campus Life`.
- Clubs, competitions, and campus activities: `Campus Activities`.
- Exams, qualifications, and certifications: `Exams & Certifications`.

17. `Education` vs. `Software & Internet`

- School courses and exams: `Education`.
- AI, software, Apps, and internet tools: `Software & Internet`.
- Computer-hardware knowledge: `Tech`.
- Classification is based on the subject being taught, not on whether the content is a tutorial.

18. `Music` vs. `Dance`

- Singing, musical instruments, and music theory: `Music`.
- Dance performances, movements, and dance instruction: `Dance`.
- For a channel combining singing and dancing, select the single `level_1` based on the main viewing motivation in recent content.

19. `Sports & Outdoors` vs. `Health & Wellness`

- Sports training, sports disciplines, fitness movements, and outdoor sports: `Sports & Outdoors`.
- Health care, supplements, oral care, and first aid: `Health & Wellness`.
- Muscle gain, fat loss, and workout plans: `Sports & Outdoors` → `Fitness`.
- Supplements and nutritional supplement products: `Health & Wellness` → `Supplements`.

20. `Sports & Outdoors` vs. `Travel`

- Hiking, camping, mountaineering, and outdoor challenges as the core themselves: `Sports & Outdoors` → `Outdoor Adventure`.
- Destinations, routes, scenery, and travel experiences as the core: `Travel`.

21. `Automotive` vs. `Casual Vlogs`

- Car reviews, repairs, modifications, parts, and motorcycles: `Automotive`.
- Occasional driving during ordinary daily life: `Casual Vlogs`.
- Road-trip content focused on destinations: `Travel`.
- Road-trip content focused on vehicle performance and driving experience: `Automotive`.

22. `Pets & Animals`

- Dogs: `Dogs`.
- Cats: `Cats`.
- Wild or ordinary animals other than cats and dogs: `Other Animals`.
- Animal rescue and protection: `Animal Welfare`.
- Pet news: `Pet News`.
- Household pets such as birds, hamsters, and reptiles: `Other Pets`.
- Pet food, toys, cages, and supplies: `Pet Supplies`.

Pet-supplies reviews should select `Pet Supplies`; do not select `Food Reviews`, `Home`, or `Consumer Electronics`.

23. `General Humanities & Society`

Select this category only when the channel primarily centers on:

- Art
- Culture
- Historical culture
- Literary culture
- Museums
- Traditional culture
- Sociocultural phenomena
- Analysis of artworks

The corresponding `level_2` may only be `Arts & Culture`.

Do not automatically select this category merely because the content is educational or knowledge-based.

19. Percentage Rounding Rules
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

All of the following fields must use integers:

- percentage
- male
- female
- active_subscriber_ratio

They must not contain:

- Decimals
- Percent signs
- Numeric strings

It is recommended to calculate decimals internally first and then round using the largest remainder method.

After rounding, recheck the total.

If the total is not 100:

- `audience_region`: preferentially adjust the item whose `region` value is `Other`.
- `audience_language`: if an item whose `language` value is `Other` exists, preferentially adjust that item; otherwise, adjust the language item with the largest share.
- `audience_age_gender`: adjust the age-gender cell with the largest original decimal remainder.
- `top_5_distribution`: preferentially adjust the item whose `tag` value is `Other`.

Do not output totals of 99, 101, or any other value.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
20. Mandatory Estimation Method When Information Is Insufficient
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Every field must be completed.

When information is insufficient, estimate in the following priority order:

1. Direct statements from the channel or creator.
2. Information repeated across multiple videos.
3. Video titles and descriptions.
4. Subtitles or transcripts.
5. Recurring on-screen people and voices, except that these must not decide `creator_gender` or `creator_age_range`.
6. Languages and context in recent comments.
7. Channel content topics and video formats.
8. Upload frequency, views, and interaction.
9. A reasonable audience structure for the same-language market and content type.
10. The most conservative channel-specific estimate.

For `creator_gender` and `creator_age_range`, "completed" means following sections 7 and 8. Do not complete those two fields from appearance, voice, a name, or a content niche.

Do not fabricate that “the channel explicitly stated” a fact merely to complete a field.

Inference is allowed, but it must come from the permitted YouTube signals.

Insufficient evidence must not cause you to:

- Use `unknown`
- Output an empty value
- Skip an object
- Output an explanation
- Copy the previous channel's result
- Apply an exactly identical percentage template
